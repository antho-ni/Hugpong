The problem

Farmers grow the coffee. Middlemen set the price.

Small coffee farmers in the uplands often sell through traders. They take the price they are offered, and the cup you buy in the city costs many times what the farmer was paid for the beans.

  • Few buyersFarms are far from town, so one trader can end up as the only option.
  • Shifting pricesThe price paid per kilo can change from one season to the next, which makes planning hard.
  • No sayFarmers rarely see where their coffee ends up or what it sells for.

The idea

Hugpong means joining together. So we cut out the middle.

Hugpong Kape is a student-run coffee shop built around one partnership between university students and highland coffee growers.

  1. Farmers harvest and dry

    Partner farming groups pick, process and dry their beans on their own land.

  2. We buy direct, at a posted price

    The shop pays the farming group directly. The price per kilo is written down and shared with you.

  3. You drink, they get their share

    Every cup carries a fixed share back to the farmers, printed on your receipt.

Why it is worth your cup

Good coffee with a clear conscience.

Fair-price buying

Beans are bought above the usual trader price, and the price is public.

    Questions

    Things you might ask.

    Is Hugpong Kape a real shop?

    No. It is a concept created for a class project, so the prices, percentages and farmer profiles are examples.

    Is the University of St. La Salle part of this?

    The idea is inspired by a university and farmer partnership, but this page is a student concept. It is not an official project of the university and does not use its name or logo as a partner.

    Where would the beans come from?

    From partner farming groups in the Negros Occidental uplands, bought directly and delivered as dried beans.

    How is the 30% share decided?

    In this concept it is a fixed share written into the agreement with each farming group. A real shop would set the number together with the farmers.
